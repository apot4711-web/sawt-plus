Sawt+ turns your Android phone into a wireless microphone for a Linux computer. The phone captures its microphone and streams 48 kHz mono PCM over the local Wi-Fi network to a small headless daemon on the PC, which exposes it as a PipeWire or PulseAudio source.

Features:

* Wi-Fi first: both devices only need to be on the same local network.
* Device discovery over mDNS, avahi, and an active network scan.
* Pair once with a short code; later connections are automatic.
* USB/ADB fallback when Wi-Fi is unavailable.
* Control from the terminal or from an optional local web page.
* Input level and mute controls on the phone and on the computer.
* Arabic and English interface with Material 3 and RTL support.

The computer side is required: it creates the virtual microphone and can be controlled with the sawt command-line client or its local browser interface at 127.0.0.1:8787.

Requirements:

* Android 7.0 (API 24) or newer.
* Linux with PipeWire or PulseAudio and pactl in PATH.
* Both devices on the same local network for Wi-Fi mode, or USB debugging for the ADB fallback.

Sawt+ is based on the original open-source audiosource project by gdzx. It is a separate project and does not replace the original app.
